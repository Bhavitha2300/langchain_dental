Plaque is a sticky film of bacteria that forms on your teeth. When you eat, especially sugary and starchy foods, these bacteria feed on the leftovers. They produce acids and other waste products, which can harm your teeth and gums.

Now, when plaque is not removed by brushing and flossing, it can start to irritate the gums. Here's how that happens:

1. **Bacteria Growth**: The bacteria in plaque multiply quickly. The more plaque there is, the more bacteria there are.

2. **Inflammation**: The body’s immune system detects the bacteria and responds by sending white blood cells to the gums. This response can cause **inflammation**, which is when the gums become red, swollen, and sometimes bleed.

3. **Gingivitis**: When the gums are inflamed due to plaque, it leads to a condition called gingivitis. Symptoms include swollen gums, redness, and bleeding, especially when brushing or flossing.

If plaque continues to build up and gingivitis is not treated, it can progress to a more severe condition called periodontitis, which can damage the bone that supports your teeth.

In summary, plaque causes gingivitis by irritating the gums through the action of harmful bacteria, leading to inflammation. That's why it's crucial to brush and floss regularly to keep plaque at bay!
